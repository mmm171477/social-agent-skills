# YouTube playbook — decisions, not rules of thumb

## 1. Package before you produce
Write these four lines before the script. If you cannot, the idea is not ready.
1. **Viewer:** who exactly, and what are they stuck on?
2. **Promise:** what will they know or be able to do by the end?
3. **Click reason:** what curiosity or payoff makes the title worth tapping?
4. **Proof:** what in the video delivers the promise within the first part?

## 2. Title and thumbnail rubric (score 0–2 each, ship at 8+ of 10)
| Test | 0 | 2 |
|---|---|---|
| Clear in 2 seconds on a phone | needs reading twice | instant |
| Promise matches the video | clickbait gap | exact |
| Thumbnail adds, not repeats | same words | new information |
| One focal point | busy | single subject |
| Distinct from the last 5 videos | similar | recognisably new |

## 3. Retention reading guide
- A drop in the opening: the promise did not match the packaging. Fix the first lines, not the thumbnail.
- A mid-video cliff: a section answered the question too early or wandered. Cut or reorder.
- A flat tail: the ending is fine; do not over-edit.
Judge by retention shape per video, not one average. State the window and sample size; small samples are hints, not conclusions.

## 4. Shorts from long videos
Pick moments that stand alone with no setup. Re-hook the first line for a viewer who knows nothing. Do not paste a clip with its original intro.

## 5. Description and metadata
Lead with the promise; put links after it; chapters as searchable phrases; disclose sponsorships and affiliate links. Do not stuff keywords.

## 6. Experiments
Change one thing per test (title or thumbnail, not both). Log: hypothesis, change, date, window, result, decision. Use `analytics-definitions.md` for metric meanings.

## 7. Never
Buy views, fabricate numbers, claim API access that the host does not expose, or publish without showing the exact asset and getting approval.
